**09:15 - 09:35**\\
**NVIDIA Vera: A Microarchitecture-Level Comparison with Grace, Turin, and Granite Rapids**

Joseph Moore, Thomas Green, Simon McIntosh-Smith\\
_Bristol Centre for Supercomputing, University of Bristol, England_

_The majority of new HPC systems are centered around NVIDIA, AMD, or Intel CPUs. Vera is NVIDIA’s latest data-center CPU, including its first custom core design, Olympus. This paper investigates Vera in comparison to AMD Turin and Intel Granite Rapids, using NVIDIA Grace as a baseline. We navigate up the performance ladder, from microbenchmarks that isolate performance metrics, through NAS kernels and miniapps, to the single-socket performance of a range of scientific codes._

_We find that Vera achieves the best performance of the architectures evaluated in most codes tested, and that this gain is primarily attributed to its high memory bandwidth, effective Spatial Multithreading implementation, and low-latency memory subsystem aided by the architecture’s predictive structures. While Vera represents a major leap over its predecessor, we show that its comparatively modest floating-point performance trails Intel and AMD in a small number of compute-bound workloads reflecting NVIDIA’s GPU focus for such workloads._
